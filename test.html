The Site

One footprint, two characters

From above, the plan reads like an open hand: a glass-fronted office wing and sunken rotunda to the north, a ribboning exhibition hall and formal parterre garden to the south, joined by a single processional axis toward the fountain court.

The campus is organized as a walk rather than a floor plan. Visitors arrive along a tree-lined drive, pass a bronze fountain roundabout, and choose between the working wing — offices, a roof terrace, café tables under string light — or the public wing, where the galleries curve like a drawn ribbon around a sequence of internal courts.

At the centre, a circular rotunda lets daylight fall straight into the lower gallery, while the southern wing unfurls into an S-shaped enfilade of exhibition rooms, each opening onto clipped hedges and gravel parterres outside. The composition resolves at a long reflecting pool fronting the main entrance, lit from below at dusk.

The Galleries

A building you walk in a loop

Each wing has its own rhythm of light, material, and pace — from the glazed working floors at the entrance to the curved exhibition halls that close the visit.

N

The Studio Wing

Offices & Terrace
A glazed, light-filled block holding studios and café seating, stepped back behind a hedge-lined roof terrace where staff and visiting designers cross paths between meetings.
R

The Rotunda

Lower Courtyard
A sunken circular court ringed in poured stone, drawing visitors down a half-level from the entrance plaza into a quiet, top-lit room before the main galleries begin.
S

The Ribbon Galleries

Exhibition Hall
An unbroken curved wall traces an S through the southern block, opening into a sequence of double-height rooms hung with rotating exhibitions and a central sculpture court.
F

The Forecourt

Entry & Pool
A long lit fountain leads from the street to the museum doors, framed by clipped cypress and shallow basin jets — the building's formal handshake with the city.
The Gardens

Four parterres, one statue

South of the galleries, clipped boxwood is cut into matching scroll patterns around a central bronze, echoing the museum's own curved walls in something that can be tended by hand.

  • Boxwood hedges trimmed into mirrored scrollwork, raked gravel infill
  • A central pedestal statue marking the cross-axis of the garden
  • Lantern-lit pathways guiding visitors toward the entrance pool
  • Cypress columns framing the final approach to the museum doors

Plan Your Visit

Arrive, wander, stay

The campus is designed to be entered slowly — by car along the tree-lined drive, or on foot through the gardens straight to the fountain court.

Hours

Daily, 10–18

Last entry one hour before close. The rotunda courtyard remains lit through the evening.

Arrival

By Drive or Foot

38 marked bays along the tree-lined service road, with step-free access through the south garden.

Grounds

Open at Dusk

Garden paths and the fountain forecourt stay lit and open to the public after the galleries close.

Groups

Guided Walks

Small-group tours trace the full loop, from the studio terrace to the ribbon galleries and gardens.
